## 3. Marketing and communications today (tasks A-03, A-04)

The questionnaire of **Corporate Marketing & Communications** is the one that matters most for DCC. It is the only department that publishes the Group's public voice, and it is where decision D-16 (start the pilot with Corporate) comes from.

**People.** Seven people are listed, in these roles: the Corporate Marketing Manager, a Digital Marketing Head, two Marketing Experts, a Content Writer, a Graphic Designer, and a Market Intelligence and Customer Insight Officer. Six of the seven rows are still marked "Needs decision" and one "In progress": the questionnaire is **not finished**.

**What they handle.** 78 record types in 14 categories (planning, digital, branding, communications, events, finance, reporting, legal and others).

| Route                                                | Record types | Which                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "No approval - owner publishes directly"             | 43           | **all** digital (8), branding (7) and communications (5) types, plus reporting, intelligence, sales support and admin |
| A unified approval chain                             | 34           | planning, events, finance, legal, policy and CSR types                                                                |
| Serial approval (several people one after the other) | 1            | correspondence                                                                                                        |

**The most important finding.** Today the content calendar, corporate communications, press releases, media briefs, social media reports and brand assets are all marked **"no approval, the owner publishes directly"**. Public communication is the one thing that is not checked by anyone else before it goes out. That is exactly the gap DCC closes.

**How approval works where it exists.** Every approval route is the same two steps: a **review** (completeness and compliance, by a Marketing Expert or the manager), then a **final approval** by the Corporate Marketing Manager **or a delegated approver** (this appears on 32 of the 37 approval steps). Of 76 route steps, 39 are reviews and 37 are approvals. Some routes add signatures: manager then finance, manager then the COO, or manager, senior marketing manager, legal, COO. The words "authority to confirm" and, on **every one of the 76 steps, "SLA: to confirm"**, show that the real approval authority and the time limits have **not been decided yet**.

## 4. What this means for DCC (the lead's reading)

1. **Roles map almost one to one.** Content Writer and Graphic Designer are DCC content creators. Marketing Experts are content reviewers. The Corporate Marketing Manager is the company marketing manager (reviews, approves, publishes). The Digital Marketing Head, who already owns the social media report, is the natural publisher and inbox owner. The COO is the company executive who signs larger items.
2. **Separation of duties can work in the pilot.** DCC needs at least three different people (write, approve, publish). The head-office marketing team lists seven. The pilot entry condition on people is met on paper, once the six open rows are confirmed.
3. **Two steps, not "N approvals".** The real routine is review, then approval, in that order, with a delegate allowed. DCC's rule today is "how many different people, from which roles", with comments from reviewers but no enforced order and no delegation. **Whether DCC needs ordered stages and delegation is a question for the marketing lead, with this evidence.** It answers part of what was asked about "levels" and "time limits".
4. **Time limits are unknown, not missing code.** No department has given a response time. Building a time limit before the number exists would invent a business rule (project rule: do not invent business rules).
5. **The communications and digital categories are the first rules to enter.** When the approval matrix is signed, the kinds of content in DCC (campaign, crisis, financial statement, group announcement, routine) should be checked against the Marketing categories above, so both systems speak the same words.
6. **The first approval matrix is half-written.** The Marketing routes are a good starting point for task A-05, but they are for documents, not for posts. Someone must say which of these routes also apply to a post on a social network.
